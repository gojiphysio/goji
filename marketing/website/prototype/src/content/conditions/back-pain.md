Back pain is one of the most common reasons people see a physiotherapist — and one of the most successfully treated. Whether your pain came on suddenly, has been building over time, or keeps recurring, a thorough assessment helps identify what is driving it and what will actually help.

## Common causes of lower back pain

**Muscle and soft tissue strain** — often triggered by a specific movement (lifting, twisting, bending) or by sustained posture (prolonged sitting, desk work). Pain is typically localised to the back and improves with movement and position change.

**Disc-related pain** — the intervertebral discs can become irritated or bulge, sometimes pressing on nearby nerves. Symptoms may include back pain, leg pain (sciatica), numbness or tingling into the foot.

**Sustained posture and movement habits** — many people develop pain gradually from the cumulative load of how they sit, stand, and move each day. This is often very responsive to physiotherapy.

**Pregnancy-related back pain** — back pain is extremely common during pregnancy, due to hormonal changes, postural shifts, and the growing weight of the baby. Pelvic girdle pain (PGP) is a related but distinct condition also well managed with physiotherapy.

**Postpartum pain** — back pain that starts in pregnancy can persist after birth. The demands of carrying and feeding an infant — often in suboptimal positions — can also generate new back pain.

**Sports and activity demands** — repetitive loading (running, lifting, team sports) can cause stress-related injuries to the vertebrae, muscles, or facet joints.

**Persistent back pain** — pain that has lasted more than three months is classified as chronic. This requires a different approach to acute back pain, and physiotherapy remains one of the most evidence-supported treatments.

## What does a physiotherapy assessment include?

Your physiotherapist will take a detailed history of your pain — when it started, what makes it better or worse, whether it travels into your leg, and how it affects your daily life. This is followed by a physical assessment including:

- Observation of posture and movement
- Range of motion and loading tests
- Neurological screening if indicated (reflexes, sensation, strength)
- Specific orthopaedic tests relevant to your presentation

The assessment guides the treatment approach. You will leave with a clear explanation of what is contributing to your pain and a plan for addressing it.

## Do I need a scan?

Imaging (X-ray, MRI) is not always necessary — and not always helpful. Most acute back pain does not require a scan. Scans are most useful when there are specific "red flag" symptoms, when symptoms are not improving as expected, or when a diagnosis will change management.

Your physiotherapist can advise whether imaging is appropriate and refer you to the relevant specialist if needed.

## Back pain in pregnancy

Back pain is extremely common in pregnancy and should not simply be accepted as inevitable. Physiotherapy can:

- Identify whether the pain is musculoskeletal back pain or pelvic girdle pain (which are managed differently)
- Provide hands-on treatment, exercise prescription, and load management advice
- Guide you on safe activity and posture through each trimester
- Prepare the body for labour and birth

## Common questions

**Should I rest or keep moving?** In most cases, keeping gently active is better than bed rest. Complete rest can cause deconditioning and prolong recovery. Your physiotherapist will advise on appropriate activity levels for your specific situation.

**Will I need surgery?** Most back pain, including disc-related pain, resolves with conservative management. Surgery is considered only when conservative treatment has not helped or when there are specific indications (for example, significant neurological deficit).

**What if my pain keeps coming back?** Recurrent back pain is very common. It is also very amenable to physiotherapy — understanding the factors driving recurrence allows a more targeted approach to preventing it.
