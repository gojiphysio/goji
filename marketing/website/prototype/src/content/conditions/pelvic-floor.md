The pelvic floor is a group of muscles, ligaments, and connective tissue at the base of the pelvis. It supports the bladder, bowel, and uterus — and plays a central role in continence, sexual function, and core stability.

Most people only think about the pelvic floor after something goes wrong. But pelvic floor physiotherapy is effective for a wide range of concerns, and many of them are far more common — and more treatable — than people realise.

## Signs that pelvic floor physiotherapy may help

**Stress incontinence** — leaking with coughing, sneezing, laughing, jumping, or lifting. This is not an inevitable part of having children, and it is not something you just have to live with.

**Urgency and urgency incontinence** — a sudden, difficult-to-defer urge to urinate, sometimes with leaking before reaching the toilet.

**Pelvic organ prolapse** — a heaviness, pressure, or dragging sensation in the pelvis; sometimes a feeling of "something coming down". Prolapse affects roughly 1 in 3 women who have given birth, and physiotherapy is a first-line treatment.

**Pelvic pain** — persistent pain in the lower abdomen, tailbone, hips, or between the legs. This includes pain that is worse during your period, during or after sex, with prolonged sitting, or when using tampons.

**Sexual discomfort** — pain with penetration (dyspareunia), inability to tolerate penetration (vaginismus), or loss of sensation.

**Bowel concerns** — constipation, difficulty fully emptying, urgency, or bowel leaking.

**Postnatal recovery** — symptoms after vaginal birth or caesarean section, including scar tissue discomfort, abdominal weakness, and return to exercise.

## What does a pelvic floor assessment involve?

A Goji pelvic floor assessment typically covers:

- A full discussion of your history, symptoms, and goals before any examination
- External assessment of posture, breathing, and movement
- Internal examination (with your informed consent) to assess muscle tone, strength, coordination, and sensation
- Functional testing relevant to your concern — for example, a cough test or squatting assessment

The examination is guided by you throughout. If you are not comfortable with an internal examination, external assessment and treatment is still valuable and we can progress at your pace.

## Pelvic floor care across the lifespan

Pelvic floor issues are not only a postnatal concern. We see women at every life stage:

- **During pregnancy** — managing pelvic girdle pain, leaking, and preparing for birth
- **Postnatal** — recovering from birth, addressing diastasis recti, returning to exercise
- **Outside pregnancy** — managing endometriosis-related pain, interstitial cystitis, vaginismus, or chronic pelvic pain
- **Perimenopause and menopause** — hormonal changes affect pelvic floor tissue; symptoms often increase and physiotherapy can help
- **Any age** — bladder and bowel concerns, prolapse management, sexual health

## Common questions

**Are Kegel exercises enough?** Not always. Many people with pelvic floor dysfunction have overactive or hypertonic (too-tight) muscles — and Kegels can make this worse. Others have coordination problems, not just weakness. An assessment clarifies what approach is right for you.

**Will I need surgery?** Physiotherapy is a recommended first-line treatment for stress incontinence and pelvic organ prolapse before considering surgery. Many people find significant improvement with conservative management.

**How long does it take?** This varies by condition and individual. Some people notice improvement after a few sessions; chronic conditions or complex presentations may take longer. Your physiotherapist will discuss realistic expectations with you at your first appointment.

**Is it only for women who have had children?** No. Pelvic floor concerns affect women who have never been pregnant, men, and children too. Anyone with bladder, bowel, or pelvic pain concerns is welcome.
