The neck and shoulder are closely interconnected — pain in one area often involves the other, and the source of symptoms is not always where the pain is felt. A thorough physiotherapy assessment identifies what is actually driving your symptoms rather than treating only the area that hurts.

## Common causes

**Sustained posture — "tech-neck"** — prolonged time looking at a screen, particularly when the screen is below eye level or the head is forward-posture, places significant load on the structures of the cervical spine. Neck stiffness, upper back tightness, and headaches are the common results.

**Rotator cuff irritation or injury** — the rotator cuff is a group of four muscles around the shoulder joint that stabilise and move the shoulder. Pain with reaching overhead, behind the back, or lying on the affected side is a common presentation.

**Shoulder impingement** — a descriptive term for pain with certain shoulder movements, often related to the structures passing through the subacromial space. Physiotherapy is a well-supported first-line treatment.

**Post-natal changes** — the positions required for feeding, carrying, and caring for a newborn — often sustained for many hours — place significant demand on the neck, shoulders, and upper back. New parent shoulder and neck pain is very common and very treatable.

**Referred pain from the cervical spine** — disc irritation or facet joint problems in the neck can refer pain into the shoulder, arm, or hand. Numbness, tingling, or weakness in the arm suggests nerve involvement and warrants prompt assessment.

**Headaches** — cervicogenic headache (headache arising from the neck) is often misidentified or undertreated. Physiotherapy is effective when the headache originates from the upper cervical spine.

## Tech-neck — a closer look

"Tech-neck" describes the pattern of neck and upper back symptoms associated with sustained use of phones, tablets, and laptops. The head in a forward position increases the effective weight on the cervical spine significantly — a head at 60 degrees of forward flexion creates around five times the load of a neutral head position.

Symptoms include:
- Neck pain and stiffness, particularly end-of-day or on waking
- Upper back tightness between the shoulder blades
- Headache at the base of the skull
- Shoulder tension that does not fully resolve

Physiotherapy addresses this through joint mobilisation, soft tissue treatment, posture education, and a targeted exercise programme.

## Shoulder assessment at Goji

A shoulder assessment includes:

- Full history of the onset, aggravating and easing factors, and your activities
- Range of motion and strength testing
- Specific orthopaedic tests for different shoulder structures
- Assessment of the neck if relevant (neck and shoulder problems frequently co-exist)
- Neurological screening if arm symptoms are present

Treatment is guided by the findings and your goals — whether you want to return to sport, resume daily tasks without pain, or manage a chronic condition more effectively.

## Common questions

**Pain when sleeping on my shoulder — is that serious?** Night pain with shoulder problems is common and rarely indicates something serious. It is worth having assessed, as it often responds well to treatment targeting the specific structure involved.

**Do I need a scan?** Most shoulder pain does not require imaging to begin treatment. Your physiotherapist will advise if a scan would change management — for example, if a rotator cuff tear is suspected or symptoms are not responding as expected.

**How long until I feel better?** This depends on the cause and duration of your symptoms. Acute presentations often improve significantly within a few sessions. More established problems may take longer, but most people see meaningful progress within 4–6 weeks of appropriate treatment.
