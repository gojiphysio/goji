Sports rehabilitation bridges the gap between an injury settling and you returning fully to training and competition - safely, confidently, and ideally more robust than before. It is also for active people who are not injured but want to move better, reduce their risk of injury, or improve how they perform. Physiotherapy assessment at Goji Physio in Lentor, Singapore identifies the physical qualities your sport demands and builds a progressive programme to meet them.

## Who is sports rehabilitation for?

Sports rehabilitation and performance work suits:

- Athletes returning from an injury who want a structured path back to sport
- People rehabilitating after a common sports injury such as an ankle sprain, hamstring strain, runner's knee, tendon pain, or an ACL injury or reconstruction
- Active people whose symptoms have settled but who do not yet feel ready for full load
- Recreational and competitive athletes wanting to reduce injury risk
- Anyone looking to improve strength, power, mobility, or movement efficiency for their sport

You do not need to be elite or injured to benefit - the same principles apply whether you run, lift, play team sports, or train for general fitness.

## What does a session at Goji involve?

Your physiotherapist will discuss your sport, training history, injury background, and goals, then assess the qualities relevant to your activity. This may include:

- Strength and power testing: identifying imbalances or deficits between sides or muscle groups
- Movement screening: how you squat, hinge, land, change direction, or perform sport-specific tasks
- Mobility and control: joint range and how well you control movement under load
- Load and capacity review: how your current training compares with the demands of your sport

You will leave with a clear picture of where you are and a structured programme that progresses your capacity toward the specific demands of your sport.

## What does a return-to-sport programme look like?

A good programme is staged. Early on, the focus is on restoring strength, mobility, and control. It then progresses to sport-specific loading - running, jumping, cutting, throwing, or lifting - before building toward full training and, where relevant, return-to-competition criteria. Rather than returning based on time alone, decisions are guided by measurable milestones, which helps reduce the chance of re-injury.

## Can physiotherapy help prevent injuries?

Injury risk can never be eliminated, but it can often be reduced. Building strength, improving movement quality, and managing training load sensibly are evidence-supported ways to help the body cope with load. A physiotherapist can help you identify and address the factors most relevant to you and your sport.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you treat men?

Yes. Goji Physio in Lentor treats men as well as women and children, for sports injuries, return to sport and performance work. Our AHPC-registered physiotherapists are trained in sports and orthopaedic rehabilitation, and our team includes a Master of Exercise Science.

### I'm not injured - can I still benefit?

Yes. Performance and injury-risk-reduction work is for active people who want to move and perform better. An assessment can highlight strengths to build on and areas that may be limiting you.

### How do you decide when I'm ready to return to sport?

Readiness is based on objective measures - strength, control, and the ability to tolerate sport-specific loads - alongside how you feel, rather than time since injury alone. Clear criteria give you and your physiotherapist confidence in the decision.

### Can physiotherapy help me get back to running after an injury?

Yes. A return-to-run plan builds strength and tolerance in stages, often starting with walking and strengthening and then moving to short run-walk intervals, guided by how the injured area responds. Your physiotherapist will tailor the stages to your injury, your running history, and your goals.

### Will I need gym-based training?

Progressive strength work is a core part of most sports rehabilitation, but programmes are tailored to your setting and equipment - whether that is a gym, home, or pitch-side. Your physiotherapist will design something realistic for you.

### How is this different from seeing a personal trainer?

Sports rehabilitation is led by a physiotherapist with clinical assessment skills, particularly valuable when you are recovering from injury or managing symptoms. It can complement work with a coach or trainer as you progress.

### How long does it take to get back to full sport?

This depends on the injury, your sport, and your starting point. Your physiotherapist will set staged milestones so you always know what you are working toward and how you are progressing.
