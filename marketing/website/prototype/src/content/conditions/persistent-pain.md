Pain that lasts beyond the expected healing time, or that keeps returning, is common and often frustrating. Persistent pain (sometimes called chronic pain) is not simply a sign that tissue is still damaged - it reflects how the whole system, including the nerves and the brain, is processing and responding to load and threat. Physiotherapy assessment at Goji Physio in Lentor, Singapore looks at the physical, movement-related, and lifestyle factors involved, so your plan targets what is actually driving your pain rather than chasing symptoms.

## Why does pain persist or keep coming back?

In the early stages, pain usually reflects tissue injury or irritation. When pain persists, the picture becomes more complex. The nervous system can become more sensitive over time, meaning normal movements or loads are interpreted as painful even when the original tissue has settled. Contributing factors often include:

- Reduced strength, mobility, or movement confidence after an injury
- Returning to full load too quickly, or avoiding movement for too long
- Sleep, stress, and general activity levels
- Previous episodes that were never fully rehabilitated

Understanding that persistent pain is real - and influenced by more than tissue damage - is an important first step, and it opens up more ways to help.

## What kinds of persistent pain does physiotherapy help with?

Physiotherapists commonly assess and manage:

- Long-standing lower back and neck pain
- Recurrent joint pain (knee, shoulder, hip)
- Arthritis-related joint pain, including knee and hip osteoarthritis
- Persistent tendon-related pain
- Pain that has lingered after an injury, surgery, or a period of reduced activity
- Widespread or fluctuating musculoskeletal pain

## Can physiotherapy help with arthritis and joint pain?

Yes. Osteoarthritis, the wear-related change that commonly affects the knees, hips, hands, and spine, can cause stiffness, aching, and pain on stairs, when walking, or after sitting for a while. Exercise and movement are core, well-supported parts of managing arthritis: they help maintain joint movement, strengthen the muscles that support the joint, and build confidence in activity. Changes seen on a scan do not always match how much pain you have, so your physiotherapist plans around how you move and what you want to keep doing, not the scan alone.

## What does a session at Goji involve?

Your physiotherapist will take a detailed history of your pain - how it started, how it behaves, what makes it better or worse, and how it affects your daily life and sleep. This is followed by an assessment that may include:

- Movement and strength testing: identifying where movement is limited, guarded, or deconditioned
- Load tolerance: understanding how much activity you can currently manage and how symptoms respond
- Contributing factors: reviewing sleep, stress, activity patterns, and past episodes
- Goal setting: clarifying the activities you want to return to

You will leave with an explanation of what is contributing to your pain and a graded plan that rebuilds capacity and confidence step by step.

## What does treatment usually involve?

Persistent pain rarely responds to a single technique. Management is typically active and progressive - graded exercise and loading, movement retraining, and education about pacing and flare-ups - sometimes supported by hands-on techniques for short-term symptom relief. The emphasis is on gradually restoring what you can do, not on eliminating every sensation before you move.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does persistent pain mean something is seriously wrong?

Not usually. Persistent pain often continues after the original tissue has healed, because the system has become more sensitive. Serious causes are uncommon, and your physiotherapist will screen for anything that needs onward medical review.

### Will exercise make my pain worse?

Appropriately graded exercise is a core, well-supported part of managing persistent pain. Some increase in symptoms during rehabilitation can be normal and safe, and your physiotherapist will guide a level of loading that challenges you without overwhelming you.

### Is exercise safe if I have arthritis?

Yes. Appropriately graded exercise is generally safe and recommended for people with arthritis, and it does not wear the joint out. Some soreness as you build up is normal, and your physiotherapist will guide the level so it challenges you without flaring your symptoms.

### Do I need a scan?

Scans often show normal age-related changes that do not explain persistent pain and can sometimes increase worry. Imaging is helpful in specific situations, and your physiotherapist will advise if a scan or medical review is warranted.

### How long will it take to improve?

This varies from person to person and depends on how long the pain has been present and the factors involved. Rather than a fixed timeline, your physiotherapist will set meaningful milestones and adjust the plan as your capacity builds.

### Can old injuries that keep flaring up be helped?

Yes. Recurring flare-ups usually point to an underlying gap in strength, control, or load management. Addressing that directly can reduce how often and how severely symptoms return.
