Good balance relies on three systems working together: the inner ear (vestibular system), vision, and the sense of position from your muscles and joints (proprioception/somatosensory), all coordinated by the brain. When any of these is affected - by an inner-ear problem, an injury, illness, or the natural changes of ageing - you may feel unsteady and giddy, making you less confident on your feet. Physiotherapy assessment at Goji Physio in Lentor, Singapore identifies which systems are contributing and builds a targeted programme to improve steadiness, strength, and confidence.

## What causes balance difficulties?

Balance problems can stem from one or several factors, including:

- **Vestibular (inner-ear) conditions:** such as BPPV, vestibular neuritis, or vestibular hypofunction
- **Reduced strength and sensation:** weakness in the legs or reduced proprioception, common with age or inactivity
- **Vision changes:** which reduce the visual input balance relies on
- **Neurological conditions and general health:** affecting how the brain integrates balance information
- **Deconditioning:** after illness, surgery, or a period of reduced activity

Because balance depends on multiple systems, assessment focuses on identifying which are involved so the programme can target them specifically.

## What are the signs balance should be assessed?

Consider an assessment if you experience:

- Feeling giddiness getting up from bed
- Spinning sensation when turning your head or rolling in bed
- Feeling dizzy in busy environments such as supermarkets or shopping centres
- Feeling unsteady, wobbly, or like you might fall
- Reaching for walls or furniture to steady yourself
- Difficulty walking on uneven ground or in the dark
- Near-falls or a fall, or growing fear of falling
- Reduced confidence that is limiting your activities

Any new balance loss with weakness, numbness, speech changes, or a sudden severe headache should be assessed medically without delay.

## What does a session at Goji involve?

Your physiotherapist will take a history of your symptoms, general health, and activities, then assess the systems that contribute to balance. This may include:

- **Balance testing:** static and dynamic balance in different conditions (eyes open/closed, firm/soft surfaces)
- **Strength and mobility:** particularly of the legs and trunk
- **Vestibular and gaze assessment:** how the inner ear and eyes support stability
- **Gait analysis:** how you walk and turn, and where steadiness reduces

You will leave with an explanation of what is affecting your balance and a personalised programme to address the contributing factors.

## How are balance difficulties treated?

Balance rehabilitation is active and progressive. Depending on the findings, it may combine balance training, leg and trunk strengthening, gait practice, and - where the inner ear is involved - vestibular rehabilitation. Exercises are graded to keep you appropriately challenged and safe, with the aim of improving steadiness and confidence over time.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is losing balance just a normal part of getting older?

Some changes are common with age, but unsteadiness is not something you simply have to accept. Balance can often be improved with targeted training, whatever your age.

### Can balance really be improved with exercise?

Yes. Balance is trainable. Progressive, specific exercises can improve steadiness, strength, and confidence, and reduce the risk of falls for many people.

### I'm afraid of falling - will exercises be safe?

Your programme is graded to your current ability and carried out with appropriate support and safety in mind. Building strength and balance is one of the best ways to reduce both falls and the fear of falling.

### Do balance problems always come from the inner ear?

No. The inner ear is one contributor, but strength, sensation, vision, and general health all play a part. Assessment clarifies which systems are involved for you.

### How long before I notice improvement?

This varies with the cause and your starting point. Your physiotherapist will set clear goals and adjust your programme as your balance improves.
