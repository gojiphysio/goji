Clinical Pilates sessions use controlled, low-impact exercises to improve strength, mobility, balance, posture and movement confidence.

Each session is guided by a physiotherapist and adapted to your current ability, exercise experience and goals. Suitable for people who are medically well and ready to exercise without requiring assessment or treatment for a new or unresolved injury.

## Who is Clinical Pilates for?

Clinical Pilates may be suitable if you want to:

- Build core and whole-body strength
- Improve posture, mobility and movement control
- Increase balance, coordination and body awareness
- Return to regular exercise after completing rehabilitation
- Support general fitness during pregnancy or after childbirth, where appropriate
- Exercise in a structured and supervised environment
- Maintain strength and mobility over time

## What is included?

A typical session may include:

- A brief check-in on how you are feeling
- A warm-up tailored to the planned exercises
- Reformer-based or mat-based exercises
- Core, upper-body and lower-body strengthening
- Mobility, balance and movement-control exercises
- Guidance on breathing, alignment and exercise technique
- Modifications and progressions based on your ability

## What is not included?

Clinical Pilates sessions do not include physiotherapy assessment or diagnosis, treatment of a new or unresolved injury, pelvic floor or musculoskeletal assessment, hands-on treatment, clinical testing, or medical advice. They should not be used as a substitute for a physiotherapy appointment.

If you develop new pain or a significant change in your symptoms, your instructor may modify or stop the session and recommend a separate physiotherapy assessment before exercise continues.

## What you can expect

You will receive:

- Clear instruction and exercise guidance
- Exercises suited to your goals and current ability
- Support with safe and effective movement technique
- Appropriate modifications and progressions
- A structured approach to improving strength, mobility and movement confidence
