A first appointment runs 55 minutes. Most of it is spent working out what is going on, not treating. You leave with an explanation of what we found, a plan, and something to do before the next session.

People often arrive expecting either a massage or a telling-off about posture. It is neither, so here is the actual shape of it.

## Before you arrive

Wear or bring clothing you can move in. For a knee or hip problem that means shorts; for a shoulder or back problem, something sleeveless or a vest top helps. You do not need a doctor's referral to see a physiotherapist in Singapore, and you do not need scans. If you happen to have recent imaging or a specialist letter, bring it, but it is not a requirement and the assessment does not depend on it.

If you are coming for a pelvic health appointment, you can come as you are. Nothing internal happens without a separate conversation and your agreement.

## The conversation, which is most of it

The first stretch is questions, and they go wider than the sore part. What happened and when. What makes it worse and what settles it. How it behaves across a day. What you were doing before this started and what you are not able to do now. Sleep, prior injuries, medical history, and for pelvic health appointments, bladder and bowel history.

This is not small talk. Most of the diagnostic information in musculoskeletal physiotherapy comes from the history rather than the hands-on part. It also establishes what actually matters to you, which is what the plan gets built around. "Able to carry my toddler up the stairs" and "back to netball" are different goals and lead to different programmes.

## The physical assessment

Then we look at how you move. Depending on the problem, that includes:

- Watching functional movements, so squatting, bending, reaching, walking, hopping
- Testing joint range and how it feels at end range
- Strength testing the relevant muscle groups
- Specific tests to include or exclude particular structures
- Hands-on assessment of the joint and soft tissue

For pelvic health appointments this may include an internal vaginal assessment, which gives the clearest information about pelvic floor function. It is offered and explained, never assumed, and you can decline it and still have a thorough assessment.

## What you get told

Before you leave, you should be able to answer three things: what we think is going on, what is going to help, and roughly how long it is likely to take to know whether it is working. If any of those are unclear, ask, because that is the part that makes the rest of it work.

Sometimes the honest answer to the first one is a shortlist rather than a single diagnosis, and the plan is built to narrow it down. That is a normal outcome of a first visit, not a failure of one.

## What you leave with

Usually some hands-on treatment in the same session if it is appropriate, and always a home programme. The programme is deliberately short. Three or four exercises that get done beat a dozen that do not, and we would rather set something that fits into an actual week.

We will also tell you what to expect between now and next time, including what a normal amount of post-session soreness looks like and what would be a reason to contact us sooner.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a doctor's referral?

No. Physiotherapists in Singapore are first-contact practitioners, so you can book directly. If we think you need medical review or imaging, we will say so and help you arrange it.

### How many sessions will I need?

It depends entirely on the problem, and anyone who gives you a firm number before assessing you is guessing. What we can do at the first visit is tell you when we should expect to see a change, and review honestly at that point.

### Will it hurt?

Assessment involves reproducing your symptoms at times, so you may feel the familiar pain during certain tests. That is information, not damage. Treatment should be tolerable, and you can stop at any point.

### What should I wear?

Something you can move in and that allows access to the area involved. Shorts for lower limb and back, a vest top or something sleeveless for neck and shoulder.

### Can I bring my baby or child with me?

Yes. Plenty of our appointments happen with a baby in the room, and the clinic is set up for it.
