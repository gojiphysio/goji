At any point after birth: weeks, months or years later. Many mothers come after their postnatal check-up, which in Singapore is usually at 4-6 weeks ([KKH](https://www.kkh.com.sg/symptoms-treatments/preparing-for-an-elective-planned-caesarean-section)), but there is no deadline. In our own notes from June to October 2026, more than 6 in 10 postnatal clients first came more than three months after giving birth, and the largest group came between six and twelve months.

If something is bothering you sooner, such as leaking, pain, or heaviness in the vagina, you do not need to wait for the six-week mark.

## When do mums actually first come in?

We counted from our own clinical notes. A script read each client's first note in memory, picked out how long after birth it was written ("12 weeks postpartum", "PP 4/12" and so on), and printed totals only. 34 postnatal clients had that timing recorded at their first visit.

| Time since birth at first visit | Clients | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Under 6 weeks | <5 | |
| 6-12 weeks | 7 | 21% |
| 3-6 months | 9 | 26% |
| 6-12 months | 13 | 38% |
| 1 year or more | <5 | |

Counts under five are hidden so no one can be identified. These are 34 people at one clinic, so read the shares as a rough picture rather than a rule.

## Is six weeks too early?

No. The postnatal check-up is a review with your doctor; a physiotherapy assessment looks in detail at the pelvic floor and tummy muscles, and the two can sit side by side. NICE recommends that women are encouraged to do pelvic floor muscle training during routine postnatal care ([NICE NG210](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng210/chapter/Recommendations)), and an assessment around six to eight weeks is a reasonable point to check how that is going.

Some things should not wait for any physio appointment and need a doctor straight away, such as heavy bleeding, a wound that is opening or looks infected, or chest pain. Our [postpartum recovery checklist](/blog/postpartum-recovery-checklist/) lists the urgent warning signs.

## Is it too late if my baby is already one?

No. Recovery questions stay assessable and treatable long after the first year, and pelvic floor muscle training is recommended as a first treatment however long symptoms have been there. In our numbers, coming in at six to twelve months was the most common pattern, often when someone wanted to get back to exercise, running or work and found something was holding them back.

## What is each stage good for?

| When | What an appointment often focuses on |
|---|---|
| First 6 weeks | Gentle pelvic floor work, caesarean or perineal recovery, comfortable feeding and lifting positions, or an earlier check if something is worrying you |
| 6-12 weeks | A full pelvic floor and tummy muscle assessment, leaking or heaviness, a plan for building back activity |
| 3-12 months | Getting back to exercise, running or lifting; back, wrist or pelvic pain from carrying and feeding; a tummy gap that has not narrowed |
| A year or more | Leaking, heaviness or pain that never fully settled, or preparing for another pregnancy |

[What happens at your first physiotherapy visit](/blog/what-happens-at-your-first-physiotherapy-visit/) walks through the appointment itself, and a [Mummy and Baby assessment](/services/mummy-baby-assessment/) covers your recovery and your baby's development in one visit.

## When do women start prenatal physio?

Prenatal physiotherapy is suitable at any stage of pregnancy. In our notes, 33 clients had a pregnancy stage recorded at their first visit:

| Stage at first visit | Clients | Share |
|---|---|---|
| First trimester (up to 13 weeks) | 8 | 24% |
| Second trimester (14-27 weeks) | 7 | 21% |
| Third trimester (28 weeks on) | 18 | 55% |

More than half first came in the third trimester. That is often when aches, pelvic girdle pain or pelvic floor symptoms appear, and 32-36 weeks is when a birth preparation session is most useful. Our [prenatal physiotherapy](/services/prenatal-physiotherapy/) page covers what that session includes, and [perineal massage before birth](/blog/perineal-massage-before-birth/) covers one part of it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a referral or my doctor's go-ahead?

You do not need a referral to book. If you have a high-risk pregnancy, we coordinate with your obstetric team before going ahead.

### Can I bring my baby?

Yes. If travelling with a newborn is the hard part, postnatal assessments are also available as a home visit, and a [Mummy and Baby assessment](/services/mummy-baby-assessment/) combines your recovery check with a developmental check for your baby.

### How were these numbers worked out?

A script counted keywords and dates in our clinical notes in memory and printed totals only, with counts under five hidden. No one read notes to write this post, and no note text, names or dates were stored.

### Should I come before my postnatal check-up if I'm leaking?

If leaking is your only concern and it is improving, six to eight weeks is a reasonable point to be assessed. If it is not improving, or there is pain, heaviness or a feeling of something bulging, there is no reason to wait.
