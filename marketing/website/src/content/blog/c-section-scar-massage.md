Wait until the wound has fully closed, with no scabs or open areas, and your doctor or midwife is happy with how it has healed. NHS hospital leaflets say that's usually six to eight weeks after a caesarean ([Kingston and Richmond NHS](https://www.kingstonandrichmond.nhs.uk/patients-and-families/patient-leaflets/scar-massage-after-caesarean)). Then start gently around the scar for five to ten minutes at a time, and work up to firmer techniques over days or weeks. The research behind scar massage is thin, so think of it as something that can help with tightness and sensitivity rather than a sure fix.

## Does scar massage actually work?

Honestly, we don't know as much as we'd like. Even the Kingston and Richmond NHS leaflet that teaches it says not much research has been done on how well it works, though some people find it helpful.

A review of scar massage studies found ten papers covering 144 people, with very different routines and outcome measures ([Shin and Bordeaux, 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22093081/)). The authors called the evidence weak. They did see more improvement in scars from surgery than in burn or injury scars: 27 of the 30 surgical scars improved in appearance or on a scar rating scale.

For caesarean scars specifically, one trial treated 28 women who had ongoing pain after a C-section with four sessions of hands-on soft tissue work ([Wasserman et al., 2018](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/55106)). Pain and scar mobility improved in both groups, whether they had lighter massage and skin rolling or deeper scar work added on. It's small, and there was no untreated group to compare with.

So it's low-risk, it's cheap, and it can help some people feel more comfortable with their scar. It isn't proven to change how your scar heals.

## When is your scar ready for massage?

Your scar is ready when the skin has completely closed. St George's Hospital in London says it takes about six weeks to heal fully, and asks women to wait until there are no open areas or scabs ([St George's](https://www.stgeorges.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/THE_STM.pdf)). Massaging too early could open the wound again and let infection in ([Kingston and Richmond NHS](https://www.kingstonandrichmond.nhs.uk/patients-and-families/patient-leaflets/scar-massage-after-caesarean)).

If you're not sure, ask at your postnatal check. And if your caesarean was months or years ago, you haven't missed the window. Kingston's leaflet says it's never too late to start.

## Which signs mean you should see a doctor instead?

Don't massage a wound that looks infected. Contact your doctor, polyclinic or hospital if your wound:

- becomes more red, painful or swollen
- feels hot
- leaks pus, other discharge or foul-smelling fluid

Also get checked if you feel generally unwell, or have severe pain ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/caesarean-section/recovery/); [St George's](https://www.stgeorges.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/THE_STM.pdf)). Our [postpartum recovery checklist](/blog/postpartum-recovery-checklist/) has the full list of warning signs after birth that need a doctor urgently.

## How do you massage a C-section scar, step by step?

Get comfortable lying down or semi-sitting with pillows. A warm bath or shower first can help, and you can use any oil you're not allergic to, such as olive, coconut or almond. Aim for five to ten minutes. St George's suggests daily or every other day, and Kingston suggests at least four times a week, easing off as the scar starts to move more freely.

Work through these stages in order. Only move on when the one before feels easy, which can take days or weeks.

| Stage | What to do | What it should feel like |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Around the scar | Make small circles on the skin just above and below the scar without touching the scar itself. If it's very sensitive, start with a cotton ball. | Comfortable, maybe a bit odd |
| 2. Circles on the scar | Using the pads of two or three fingers, make small circles on and around the scar, clockwise then anticlockwise, 5 to 10 times. | A gentle stretch |
| 3. Up and down | Move the skin across the scar, up and down, with short strokes and then longer ones, 5 to 10 times. | Pulling or mild burning is normal at first |
| 4. Side to side | Rest your hand on the scar and ease it left and right along its length for 10 to 15 seconds, then the other way with your other hand. | Tighter in some spots than others |
| 5. Deeper | Press your fingers more firmly into the lower tummy and repeat the circles and strokes. Spend longer where it feels tight. | Firm, but not painful |
| 6. Skin rolling | Pinch the scar gently between thumb and finger and roll it up, down and side to side, moving along the whole scar a centimetre or two at a time. | Can take days or weeks to get to |

Sources: [St George's](https://www.stgeorges.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/THE_STM.pdf) and [Kingston and Richmond NHS](https://www.kingstonandrichmond.nhs.uk/patients-and-families/patient-leaflets/scar-massage-after-caesarean).

Let pain be your guide. A slight tingle as the tissue stretches is fine. If it hurts, stop, leave it for a few weeks and try again, and see your GP or doctor if the pain doesn't settle.

## Why does the scar feel numb or tight?

Numbness is common. KK Women's and Children's Hospital says you may have some numbness around the incision that can last for several months ([KKH](https://www.kkh.com.sg/symptoms-treatments/preparing-for-an-elective-planned-caesarean-section)). In the first few weeks the scar can also feel tender and itchy, and it often heals pink or red and slightly raised ([St George's](https://www.stgeorges.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/THE_STM.pdf)).

Tightness comes from scar tissue, which keeps forming and changing for up to two years ([Kingston and Richmond NHS](https://www.kingstonandrichmond.nhs.uk/patients-and-families/patient-leaflets/scar-massage-after-caesarean)). It isn't only in the line you can see. A caesarean goes through several layers of the tummy wall, and the tissue around and under the scar can feel stuck, or pull with certain movements ([caesarean recovery](/conditions/caesarean-recovery/)). That's why the routine above works on the skin around the scar and the layers below, and not just the line itself.

Some areas can be oversensitive while others feel numb. Starting with light touch around the scar, and only gradually working onto it, gives the area time to get used to being handled.

## Why might a physio look at your scar?

You can do this routine on your own. A physio is useful when the scar is still sore, very sensitive or tight after a few weeks of trying, when it pulls with movement, or when you're not sure you're doing it right.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will massage make my scar fade?

The NHS says a caesarean scar is usually red and obvious at first and fades with time. On darker skin it may leave a brown or white mark ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/caesarean-section/recovery/)). St George's says it can take two years to become a smooth, pale line. Some studies of surgical scars found they looked better after massage, but the evidence is weak, so it's best not to count on massage changing how your scar looks.

### Can I massage a scar from a caesarean years ago?

Yes. You can start at any time once the wound has fully healed, and it's never too late ([Kingston and Richmond NHS](https://www.kingstonandrichmond.nhs.uk/patients-and-families/patient-leaflets/scar-massage-after-caesarean)). Start at the first stage, as you would with a new scar.

### What oil or cream should I use?

Any oil you're not allergic to is fine, such as olive, coconut or almond ([St George's](https://www.stgeorges.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/THE_STM.pdf)). It's there for comfort, so your fingers glide rather than drag. Skip almond oil if you have a nut allergy.
