Yes. Goji's physiotherapists do home visits across Singapore, including in the weeks after birth. A 55-minute postpartum or pelvic floor session at home is $380. The 85-minute Mummy and Baby assessment, which looks at your recovery and your baby's movement in one visit, is $450 at home. The same appointments at our Lentor clinic are $260 and $330. You don't need a doctor's referral to book.

## Which postnatal appointments can be done at home?

Three of our women's health appointments are available as home visits, and all three suit the months after birth:

- [Postpartum physiotherapy](/services/postpartum-physiotherapy/) looks at your recovery as a whole after a vaginal or caesarean birth, including your tummy, pelvic floor, scar, any aches and pains, and getting back to exercise.
- [Pelvic floor physiotherapy](/services/pelvic-floor-physiotherapy/) is for leaking, urgency, heaviness, constipation or pain with sex.
- The [Mummy and Baby assessment](/services/mummy-baby-assessment/) covers your recovery plus a check of your baby's movement and early development, for babies up to about 12 months.

The postpartum assessment you'd have at the clinic is the same one we bring to your home.

## What happens in a postnatal home session?

It starts with a conversation. Your physio asks about your medical history, your pregnancy and birth, how recovery has gone so far, what's bothering you and what you want to get back to. Expect it to take a while. A lot of what we need comes from this part.

Then there's a physical assessment, aimed at whatever you've raised. Depending on that, it can include your posture, a check of your tummy muscles for diastasis recti, your caesarean, perineal or episiotomy scar, your pelvic floor, and how you move. The pelvic floor can be assessed by watching from the outside or, where it's appropriate, with an internal vaginal examination.

Any internal pelvic floor examination will be explained beforehand and is entirely optional. It will only be performed with your informed consent, and you may decline or stop the examination at any time without affecting your care. Our physiotherapy team is currently all-female.

The pelvic floor can also be checked with a transabdominal ultrasound. If you're hoping for that, or any other specific part of the assessment, ask when you book whether it can be done at home.

Treatment can start in the same visit. That might be hands-on work, exercises, ways to manage symptoms, or advice on getting back to daily tasks. Before your physio leaves, you should have an explanation of what they found, guidance for managing your symptoms, an exercise plan where it's needed, and a sense of what follow-up makes sense.

In a Mummy and Baby assessment, your physio also watches how your baby lies and moves on their own, checks milestones for their age, and gently looks at muscle tone and symmetry. They'll look at tummy time, carrying and positioning too. At home, that happens on your own floor with the carrier and play mat you actually use.

## When does a home visit make more sense than the clinic?

During confinement. HealthHub describes confinement as 30 days after birth for Chinese families, 40 for Indian families and 44 for Malay families, with the new mum staying home for that time ([HealthHub](https://www.healthhub.sg/well-being-and-lifestyle/pregnancy-and-infant-health/pregnancy-first-days-of-our-lives-with-baby)). If you're keeping to it, a home visit lets you start without breaking it.

After a caesarean. KKH advises not driving until you've fully recovered and your wound has completely healed, which may take up to six weeks, and not lifting anything heavier than your baby ([KKH](https://www.kkh.com.sg/symptoms-treatments/preparing-for-an-elective-planned-caesarean-section)). Getting a pram in and out of a car boot doesn't sit well with that. Our page on [caesarean recovery](/conditions/caesarean-recovery/) covers what physio can help with after the operation.

Around feeds. The NHS gives a rough guide of at least 8 to 12 feeds every 24 hours in the first few weeks ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/breastfeeding-and-bottle-feeding/breastfeeding/the-first-few-days/)). Fitting a 55-minute appointment and a return trip between them is harder than it sounds. At home there's no travel to plan around.

When getting out is the hard part for some other reason. Twins, a toddler at home, no help that day, or simply not feeling ready for the outside world yet.

## What should I have ready at home?

Not much. These help:

- A clear patch of floor about the length of a yoga mat, and a bed or sofa you can lie on.
- Clothes you can move in that let your physio see your tummy, such as leggings and a top you can lift.
- Your hospital discharge summary or any letters from your obstetrician, if they're handy. They aren't essential.
- A room with a door you can close. If family or a confinement nanny is around, you may want some privacy, especially if you choose the internal examination.
- For a Mummy and Baby assessment, the play mat, carrier or sling you normally use.
- A comfortable room temperature. HealthHub says there's no harm in using the fan or air-conditioner if it keeps you and your baby comfortable ([HealthHub](https://www.healthhub.sg/well-being-and-lifestyle/pregnancy-and-infant-health/pregnancy-first-days-of-our-lives-with-baby)).

If you can, book for just after a feed. Babies don't always go along with the plan, and that's fine.

## Frequently asked questions

### How soon after birth can I have a home visit?

At any stage. You don't have to wait for your six-week postnatal review if something is bothering you earlier, and recovery questions can still be assessed months or years after birth.

### Is a home visit suitable after a caesarean?

Yes. The postpartum assessment is designed for recovery after both vaginal and caesarean birth, and includes a scar assessment and advice on returning to activity after abdominal surgery.

### Do you come to my part of Singapore?

We do home visits across Singapore, including the East Coast. The clinic itself is at Lentor Modern.

### Can I claim a home visit on insurance?

It depends on your policy. We don't offer direct billing, so you pay first and claim from your insurer afterwards. Some insurers need a doctor's referral letter, and not every policy treats a home visit the same as a clinic session, so check before you book. Our post on [MediSave and insurance for physiotherapy](/blog/medisave-insurance-for-physiotherapy/) explains the options.
