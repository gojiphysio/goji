Surgery is often only the first step - how you rehabilitate afterwards has a major influence on how well you recover your movement, strength, and function. Post-operative physiotherapy at Goji Physio in Lentor, Singapore provides a structured, staged plan that respects your surgeon's protocol and healing timelines while progressively rebuilding what surgery and the preceding injury took away. The goal is a confident return to the activities that matter to you.

## What kinds of surgery does rehabilitation help with?

Physiotherapists commonly support recovery after:

- Joint replacements (total knee replacement, total hip replacement)
- Ligament and tendon repairs or reconstructions (e.g. ACL reconstruction, rotator cuff repair, Achilles repair)
- Arthroscopic and keyhole procedures (knee, shoulder, hip), including meniscus repair
- Spinal surgery
- Fracture fixation and orthopaedic trauma surgery
- Abdominal or pelvic surgery, where movement and core function are affected (for caesarean birth, see Caesarean Recovery and Scar Management)

Each procedure has its own healing timeline and precautions, so rehabilitation is always matched to your specific operation and your surgeon's guidance.

## What does knee or hip replacement rehabilitation involve?

Rehabilitation after a knee or hip replacement usually starts soon after surgery, with the early focus on safe walking, easing swelling and pain, and regaining movement. It then progresses to strengthening, stairs, balance, and the activities you want to return to, such as walking longer distances or getting in and out of a car. Precautions and timelines vary with the type of replacement, your surgeon's protocol, and your starting point, so your plan is built around your surgeon's guidance.

## What does a session at Goji involve?

Your physiotherapist will review your operation, any surgeon's protocol or precautions, your current stage of healing, and your goals. Assessment and treatment may include:

- Range of movement: safely restoring joint mobility within any post-surgical limits
- Strength and control: progressively rebuilding the muscles affected by surgery and reduced activity
- Swelling and pain management: strategies to keep you comfortable and moving
- Functional progression: walking, stairs, and the daily or sporting tasks you are working toward

You will leave with a clear, staged programme that matches your recovery phase and progresses as your tissues heal.

## Why does timing and staging matter?

After surgery, tissues heal in predictable phases, and loading needs to match each phase - enough to stimulate recovery, but within safe limits. Doing too much too soon can compromise a repair, while doing too little can leave you stiff and weak. A physiotherapist works within your surgeon's timelines to progress you at the right pace, with clear milestones along the way.

## Can I start rehabilitation before surgery?

Often yes. "Prehabilitation" (pre-surgery physiotherapy) - building strength, movement, and understanding before an operation - can help some people enter surgery in better condition and feel more prepared for recovery. If you have surgery planned, an early assessment can be worthwhile.

## Frequently asked questions

### When should I start physiotherapy after surgery?

This depends on the procedure and your surgeon's guidance. Some rehabilitation begins within days, while other protocols involve an initial protected period. Your physiotherapist will align with your surgeon's timeline.

### How long does knee replacement rehabilitation take?

Recovery from a knee or hip replacement is usually measured in months rather than weeks, with most early progress in the first few months and strengthening continuing beyond that. Your surgeon's protocol, your health and fitness before surgery, and your goals all influence the pace, so your physiotherapist will set staged milestones rather than a fixed date.

### Will rehabilitation be painful?

Some discomfort during recovery is normal, but rehabilitation is progressed within safe and tolerable limits. Your physiotherapist will work with you to manage pain and swelling as you rebuild movement and strength.

### Do you follow my surgeon's protocol?

Yes. Where a surgeon has provided a protocol or precautions, your programme is built around it. Where helpful, we can communicate with your surgical team to keep your recovery coordinated.

### How long does post-operative recovery take?

Timelines vary widely by procedure - some recoveries take weeks, others many months. Rather than a fixed date, your physiotherapist will set staged milestones so you can track progress.

### What if I had surgery a while ago and still feel limited?

It is often still worthwhile. Lingering stiffness, weakness, or loss of confidence after surgery can usually be assessed and addressed with a targeted programme, even months later.
