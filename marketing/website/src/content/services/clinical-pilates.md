Clinical Pilates is Pilates led by a physiotherapist rather than a fitness instructor. At Goji in Lentor, Singapore, it is a 55-minute reformer or mat session with a physiotherapist registered with the Allied Health Professions Council (AHPC), in a private 1:1 or 2:1 setting, for $180 a session.

Sessions are adapted to your goals and abilities, for example building strength, improving mobility or returning to regular exercise after rehabilitation. Clinical Pilates is an exercise service for people who are medically ready to exercise, not a physiotherapy consultation.

If you would rather work with a Pilates instructor, [Private Pilates](/services/private-pilates/) is taught at Goji by a STOTT PILATES® certified instructor.

## What is the difference between clinical Pilates and studio or reformer Pilates?

The exercises can look alike. "Reformer" is the equipment, a sliding carriage with springs, and both clinical and studio sessions can use it or the mat. The difference is who leads the session, how it starts and the setting.

| | Clinical Pilates at Goji | Private Pilates at Goji | Group studio class |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who leads | AHPC-registered physiotherapist | STOTT PILATES® certified instructor | Pilates instructor; training and certification vary by studio |
| Assessment | A brief check-in on your physical condition and goals each session; no formal physiotherapy assessment or diagnosis | A brief check-in on how you are feeling and your goals; no physiotherapy assessment | Varies by studio |
| Setting | Private 1:1 or 2:1, reformer or mat, at our Lentor clinic | Private 1:1 or 2:1, reformer or mat, at our Lentor clinic | A class shared with other participants |
| Focus | Exercise guided by clinical knowledge, for example after rehabilitation or with specific physical considerations | Strength, flexibility, body awareness and movement confidence, for beginners and experienced clients | Varies by studio and class |

Goji does not run group classes. Neither of our Pilates services replaces physiotherapy assessment or treatment: if you need that, book a physiotherapy consultation. If you're unsure which is right for you, our team can help you choose.

## Who is clinical Pilates for?

Clinical Pilates may be suitable if you want to:

- Build core and whole-body strength with physiotherapist-led guidance
- Improve posture, mobility, balance and movement control
- Return to regular exercise after completing rehabilitation
- Exercise with consideration for previous injuries or physical limitations
- Maintain strength and mobility through different life stages
- Stay active during pregnancy or after childbirth, where appropriate
- Build confidence in movement through a structured, supervised programme

The World Health Organization recommends regular muscle-strengthening activity for all age groups ([Bull et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-102955)).

Clinical Pilates is intended for individuals who are medically ready to exercise. If you have a new or unresolved injury, pain or other symptoms requiring assessment, a physiotherapy consultation may be recommended first.

## Can you do prenatal or postnatal Pilates with a physiotherapist?

Clinical Pilates may be suitable during pregnancy or after childbirth, where appropriate, and the same rule applies as for every session: it is for people who are medically ready to exercise.

What the guidelines say:

- During pregnancy: an Asia-Pacific consensus from KK Women's and Children's Hospital recommends that pregnant women with no contraindications aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity physical activity each week, and lists modified Pilates as one example of moderate-intensity activity ([Lee et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjsem-2020-000967)). The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists states that, in the absence of obstetric or medical complications or contraindications, physical activity in pregnancy is safe and desirable ([ACOG Committee Opinion 804, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1097/AOG.0000000000003772)).
- What to avoid: the same consensus advises against hot Pilates, and against lying flat on your back while exercising after the first trimester ([Lee et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjsem-2020-000967)).
- After birth: it advises resuming physical activity gradually, as soon as it is medically safe, depending on the mode of delivery and any complications ([Lee et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjsem-2020-000967)).
- Before you start: HealthHub advises checking with your doctor before you start an exercise regime, especially if you have a medical condition or problems in your past or current pregnancy ([HealthHub](https://healthhub.sg/live-healthy/pregnancy-being-fit-when-pregnant)).

If you have symptoms you would like assessed, start with [prenatal physiotherapy](/services/prenatal-physiotherapy/) or [postpartum physiotherapy](/services/postpartum-physiotherapy/). For a guide to getting back to exercise after birth, see [returning to exercise after birth](/blog/returning-to-exercise-after-birth/) and our [postpartum pathway](/pathways/postpartum/).

## Can Pilates help with back pain?

The evidence is mixed. A Cochrane review of Pilates for low back pain found no high quality evidence for any comparison, but low to moderate quality evidence that Pilates is more effective than minimal intervention for pain and disability. It found no conclusive evidence that Pilates is better than other forms of exercise, and suggested the choice can be based on preference and cost ([Yamato et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010265.pub2)).

Clinical Pilates at Goji does not include assessment or treatment of back pain. If your back pain is new, getting worse or has not been assessed, book a [musculoskeletal physiotherapy](/services/musculoskeletal-physiotherapy/) consultation first.

## What happens in a clinical Pilates session?

A typical session may include:

- A brief check-in on your physical condition and exercise goals
- A warm-up for the planned exercises
- Reformer-based or mat-based Pilates exercises
- Core, upper-body and lower-body strengthening
- Mobility, balance and movement-control exercises
- Guidance on breathing, alignment and exercise technique
- Exercise modifications and progressions informed by your physical needs

You will receive guidance from an AHPC-registered physiotherapist, exercises adapted to your physical abilities and goals, and a structured approach to improving strength and mobility.

## What does clinical Pilates not include?

Clinical Pilates is an AHPC-registered physiotherapist-led exercise service, not a physiotherapy consultation. Sessions do not include formal physiotherapy assessment or diagnosis, treatment of new or unresolved injuries, pelvic floor or musculoskeletal assessments, hands-on treatment, clinical testing or medical advice.

If you experience new pain or significant changes in your symptoms, your physiotherapist may modify or pause the session and recommend a separate physiotherapy assessment before continuing.
