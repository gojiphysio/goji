# Compliance Guardrails - Singapore (Two-Lane Model)

**Read this before publishing, boosting, or briefing anyone on any asset.** Singapore regulates how clinics and registered health professionals advertise *healthcare*. It does **not** regulate general fitness and wellness marketing the same way. The mistake to avoid is applying hospital-grade rules to a Pilates class. The mistake on the other side is making a clinical treatment claim in a "fitness" wrapper. This doc keeps the two lanes clean.

> This is practitioner guidance, not legal advice. The licensable-status question below must be verified with MOH before we rely on the freer reading for the clinical side.

## The decisive test (apply to every asset)

> **Am I advertising the treatment of a medical condition?**

- **YES** -> Clinical lane (Lane A). Be careful. Education-first.
- **NO** - it is general fitness, strength, movement, or wellbeing -> Fitness & wellness lane (Lane B). Normal consumer marketing freedom.

The *service and the claim* decide the lane, not the label. "Pilates to fix your incontinence" is Lane A even though Pilates sounds like fitness. "Build core strength and feel strong again" is Lane B.

## Which rules actually apply

Three regimes exist. How hard they bite depends on the lane and on Goji's licence status.

1. **AHPC Code of Professional Conduct** - binds every registered physiotherapist personally, always, in both lanes. Core requirement: advertising must be **truthful, accurate, and must not induce unnecessary demand** for services. It also says a practitioner must not give the impression their skills/equipment/facilities are better than others (this is what restricts superlatives and comparisons), and restricts package deals/discounts that induce unnecessary demand.
2. **Medicines (Advertisement and Sale) Act (MASA)** - restricts advertising the *treatment of an ailment, disease, injury or condition*. Bites in Lane A. Largely irrelevant to Lane B.
3. **HCSA Advertisement Regulations** - the strict list (no superlatives, no before/after, no discounts/packages in ads, etc.). These apply to *licensable* healthcare services.

> **VERIFY BEFORE LAUNCH - the pivotal open item:** Is Goji's physiotherapy currently a *licensable* service under HCSA? The sources conflict: one MOH FAQ lists physiotherapy as an example of a *non-licensable* service, while the HCSA (in force Jan 2022) expanded coverage to allied health services including physiotherapy, rolled out in phases. Whether it is licensable *now* depends on the phase. Confirm Goji's actual status with MOH. If licensable, the full HCSA strict list is mandatory for Lane A. If not, the HCSA list is best-practice for Lane A and the binding floor is AHPC + MASA.

**Why this does not block us:** our Lane A strategy is education-first and conservative either way. We never plan to lean on discounts, before/after imagery, or testimonials on the clinical side. So whichever way the licence question resolves, the clinical content we are producing already complies. We only need the answer before we would ever use a clinical-side promotion, price offer, or testimonial.

## Lane A - Clinical (physiotherapy, treating a condition)

Covers: pelvic floor dysfunction, diastasis recti, incontinence, pre/post-natal rehab framed as treatment, pain, injury, any "we treat X condition" message, and any content where a registered physio is addressing a medical problem.

**Hard NO:**
- No cure or outcome guarantees ("fix your diastasis", "cure incontinence", "pain-free guaranteed", "results in 6 weeks").
- No superlatives or laudatory claims ("best", "leading", "No.1", "the only").
- No comparative claims against other named or implied providers.
- No before/after photos in advertising (allowed only inside a private consultation).
- No unsubstantiated or embellished clinical claims - every claim defensible with evidence.
- Treat discounts/packages and testimonials as **off-limits until the HCSA licence question is resolved** (and even then, high bar - see grey zone).

**Clear YES:**
- Factual descriptions of what we assess and how a session works.
- Practitioner credentials, registration titles, special interests. This is our credibility engine.
- Education: "what is the pelvic floor", "is leaking after birth common", "how physio can help with X" (help, not cure).
- Exact prices stated plainly (no "from", no discount framing).
- Responding professionally to organic, unsolicited reviews.

**Default posture for Lane A: lead with education and clinical authority, not promotion.** Education almost always passes.

## Lane B - Fitness & Wellness (Pilates, S&C, general strength and movement)

Covers: group/private Pilates for general fitness, strength & conditioning, "return to movement", "build strength", postnatal *fitness* (as wellbeing, not treatment), classes, memberships, the studio experience, lifestyle and community content.

This is **normal consumer fitness marketing**. You have the freedoms a boutique studio or gym has:

**Allowed (subject to the floor below):**
- Promotions, intro offers, class passes, founding-member offers, trials, referral perks.
- Transformation and member-journey content, lifestyle and aspirational imagery (with consent for any real person shown).
- Stronger calls to action and offers ("Book your first class", "Join the founding cohort").
- Energetic, benefit-led copy and community-building.

**The one tripwire:** the moment a Lane B asset attaches a **clinical treatment claim** ("our Pilates treats your back pain / fixes your pelvic floor / cures..."), it crosses into Lane A and all of Lane A's rules apply. Keep fitness messaging about strength, capability, energy, and wellbeing - not about treating a diagnosed condition.

**The floor (still applies in Lane B because your face is a registered physio brand):**
- Be truthful and accurate (AHPC). Do not overpromise.
- Do not imply the *physio/clinical* service is "the best" - keep superlatives, if used at all, to the general fitness experience, and even then keep them honest.

## The grey zone - post-partum, our wedge

Post-partum is exactly where the two lanes blur, and it is our lead wedge, so handle it deliberately:

- **"Pelvic floor recovery", "healing diastasis", "treating leaking"** = Lane A. Careful, education-first, no guarantees.
- **"Rebuild your strength after birth", "move and feel like yourself again", "postnatal Pilates to get strong"** = Lane B. Free, as long as no treatment claim is attached.
- **Practical rule:** when a post-partum piece could be read either way, default to the *educational* framing (safe in both lanes) and route the call to action to a free assessment/consult rather than a clinical promise.

## Things that always apply, both lanes

- **No reposting a patient's own social post** about their experience to Goji's channels (treat as a Lane A testimonial risk).
- **No paid reviews or paid testimonial endorsements.** No incentivised reviews.
- **Genuine, unpaid educational collaborations** with creators are fine (a physio teaches on a creator's channel); a *paid review of Goji* is not. Disclose material connections.
- **Meta ad targeting:** reach mums via neighbourhood, age, parenting/lifestyle interests, and site-visitor retargeting - not health-status targeting (restricted, and Lane-A-flavoured).

## Pre-publish checklist (lane-aware)

For every asset, first decide the lane, then check:

**If Lane A (clinical):**
- [ ] No cure/outcome/time guarantee
- [ ] No superlative or comparative claim
- [ ] No before/after imagery
- [ ] No discount/package/testimonial (pending HCSA licence answer)
- [ ] Every clinical claim defensible with evidence
- [ ] Framed as education / "can help", not "will fix"

**If Lane B (fitness/wellness):**
- [ ] No clinical treatment claim attached (or it becomes Lane A)
- [ ] Claims are truthful, not overpromising (AHPC floor)
- [ ] Consent on file for any real person shown
- [ ] Offers/promotions are clean and clear

**Both lanes:**
- [ ] No reposted patient content; no paid/incentivised reviews
- [ ] If a price appears, it is exact and unembellished (Lane A) or a clearly-stated offer (Lane B)
